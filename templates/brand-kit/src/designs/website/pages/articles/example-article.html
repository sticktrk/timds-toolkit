What an article can hold

The summary field, one or two sentences. It sits under the title here and on every card that lists the article.

Short answer

The header can display a separate plain-text answer.

Everything below the header is the article's body, written in the CMS and rendered into this one wrapper. Each element here is one a body may contain, styled by the system, so a writer never picks a font or a color. Starter copy: it describes the page, and real articles replace it.

Headings and paragraphs

The title is the page's only first-level heading, so a body divides into sections under second-level headings. A paragraph may hold an inline link, strong emphasis, emphasis, and inline code.

A subsection

A third-level heading divides a section. A body goes no deeper than this.

Lists

An unordered list holds points in no particular order:

  • One point per item.
  • Short items, each a phrase or a sentence.
  • No more than a handful before the list wants headings instead.

An ordered list holds steps a reader follows in turn:

  1. Read the step.
  2. Do what it says.
  3. Move on to the next one.

Quotes and code

A quotation is set apart from the writer's own words:

A quotation from a person or a source, with who said it in the sentence before or after.

A code block keeps its spacing and scrolls inside its own box on a narrow screen:

const greeting = "Hello";
    console.log(greeting);
